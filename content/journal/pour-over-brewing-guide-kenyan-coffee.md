Kenyan coffee rewards precision. This recipe draws out its blackcurrant sweetness and keeps the acidity juicy rather than sharp.

## You'll need

- 15 g Belmont **Highland Reserve**, freshly ground medium-fine (like coarse sea salt)
- 250 g water at **93 – 94 °C**, soft and filtered
- A V60 or similar cone, paper filter, scale and timer

## The recipe

1. **Rinse** the filter with hot water and discard the water.
2. **Bloom:** pour 40 g of water, swirl gently and wait 40 seconds.
3. **First pour:** pour slowly in circles up to 150 g by 1:15.
4. **Second pour:** pour up to 250 g by 1:45.
5. **Drawdown:** give a gentle swirl and let it drain. Aim to finish at **2:45 – 3:15**.

## Adjusting

- **Sour or thin?** Grind finer, or use slightly hotter water.
- **Bitter or dry?** Grind coarser.
- **Flat?** Your water may be too hard. Try bottled water with low mineral content.

## Let it cool

Kenyan coffee opens up as it cools. Taste it at 60 °C and again at 45 °C, when the fruit and sweetness are at their most expressive.
