Buying directly from origin gives you better traceability and often better value, but the first container can feel daunting. Here is the process we walk new partners through.

## 1. Define your requirements

Grade, cup profile, volume, packaging, shipment window, destination port and preferred Incoterm. The more specific you are, the better your offer samples will match.

## 2. Cup offer samples

We send 200 – 300 g of each candidate lot with full specifications. Cup them blind against your current coffees.

## 3. Agree a contract

We confirm the lot or quality specification, price (fixed, or a differential to the ICE "C" market), quantity, shipment period, Incoterm, payment terms and arbitration rules (we use **GCA** or **ECF** standard contracts).

## 4. Approve the pre-shipment sample

Drawn from the actual bags in your container. Nothing ships until you approve it.

## 5. Shipping and documents

We book and stuff the container and send you the documents: bill of lading, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, quality and weight reports, and any certification documents.

## 6. Arrival and clearance

Your customs broker clears the coffee using our documents. We stay on hand until the container is emptied at your warehouse.

## Common pitfalls

- **Ignoring moisture.** Specify 10 – 12% and ask for GrainPro on specialty lots.
- **Booking in the wrong season.** Contract forward during the harvest to secure the best lots.
- **Unclear specs.** "AA" alone is not a quality specification. Add a minimum cup score and defect limits.
