Kenya grades its coffee primarily by **bean size and shape**, measured with perforated screens in 64ths of an inch. Size is not the same as quality, but in Kenya the two are closely linked: larger, denser beans from high-altitude farms usually cup more intensely.

## The main grades

| Grade | Screen | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| **E** (Elephant) | 18+ (very large) | Rare, large beans, often from multiple cherries fused; uneven roasting |
| **AA** | 17/18 | The flagship: dense, intense, the most sought-after grade |
| **AB** | 15/16 | Most of the crop; excellent value with classic Kenyan character |
| **PB** (Peaberry) | Round, single bean | Concentrated flavour; roasts evenly |
| **C** | Smaller than AB | Lighter body, for blends |
| **TT / T** | Light, small or broken | Separated by air or density; commercial use |

## Grade is only the starting point

Two AA lots can taste very different. Altitude, variety (SL28 and SL34 are prized), processing discipline at the washing station and drying time all matter more than a few screen sizes. That's why we **cup every lot** and offer it with a score and tasting notes rather than a grade alone.

> A great AB from a disciplined washing station will outperform a careless AA every time.

## Choosing the right grade

- **Single-origin filter programmes:** AA or top AB lots scoring 86+.
- **Espresso blends:** AB delivers Kenyan sweetness and acidity at a sensible cost per kilo.
- **Even roasting and novelty:** PB has a loyal following and looks beautiful in retail jars.
