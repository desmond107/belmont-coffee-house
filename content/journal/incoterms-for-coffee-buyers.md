Incoterms define **who pays for what, and where risk passes** from seller to buyer. Choosing the right term can save money and avoid disputes, especially on a first container.

## The terms we use most

**FOB Mombasa (Free On Board).** We clear the coffee for export and load it onto your vessel. Risk passes to you once the coffee is on board. You control the freight, which suits importers with good rates from their own forwarders.

**CFR (Cost & Freight).** We also book and pay the ocean freight to your port. Risk still passes at loading in Mombasa, so arrange your own insurance.

**CIF (Cost, Insurance & Freight).** As CFR, plus we insure the cargo, normally for 110% of the invoice value. A good choice for first-time importers.

**FCA (Free Carrier).** We hand the cleared coffee to your forwarder at an agreed place, such as our warehouse or the inland container depot. This is often a better fit than FOB for containerised cargo.

**DAP (Delivered At Place).** We deliver to your door, with import duties and taxes excluded. Available for selected markets and for private-label programmes.

## Which should you choose?

- **Experienced importers** with forwarder contracts: **FOB** or **FCA**.
- **Roasters importing directly for the first time:** **CIF**, so freight and insurance are handled for you.
- **Hospitality and retail brands:** **DAP**, for simple landed costs.

## A note on risk

Under FOB, CFR and CIF, risk passes in Mombasa, so damage at sea is the buyer's risk (and the insurer's, under CIF). Always check that your insurance covers **moisture and condensation**, the most common cause of coffee claims, and ask for GrainPro liners on specialty lots.
